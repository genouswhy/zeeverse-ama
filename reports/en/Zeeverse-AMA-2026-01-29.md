# Zeeverse / FOREST AMA Deep Dive | January 29, 2026

January 29, 2026 · Community Call · Jackal and Euler, with several players joining to ask questions · Toward the end, the hosts said they had been on the call for about an hour

This call gave players two developments to track. **Euler said he had checked during the call and found that the $VEE staked in the Ambassador Program had just unlocked. Rewards would be handled separately.** The most concrete new gameplay proposal was to **make Heist a Tribe competition lasting roughly a week, with entry fees and spending on buffs feeding the prize pool**. The UI and Stash changes were closer to release. The Forge and Great Tree were still in parallel development, and the team saw new-player retention as a prerequisite for the next expansion of content. FOREST also had its first test figures from a live trading competition.

This report follows an English transcript without line-by-line timestamps or speaker labels. Speaker attribution follows how participants address each other and hand over the floor. The official announcement sets out event rules, and the figures reported on the call are identified as such. [English transcript](../transcription/2026-01-29.txt)

## 2. Ambassador Program stakes reportedly unlocked, with no compensation planned yet for people still locked in the legacy contract

A player on mic asked whether **the principal staked in the Ambassador Program** would remain tied up. Euler initially said the team was releasing it. He then said he had checked and that it had just unlocked, told players to check their wallets, and said **100% of the principal** would be returned. The call showed no records of funds arriving in wallets, and rewards were still to be handled separately. Euler said there had been cases of inviting existing players as if they were new or creating accounts to qualify for rewards, without bringing in enough genuine referrals. The team wanted to wind down the old program and redesign ambassador status, incentives, and rules. A player suggested allowing ambassadors to join without staking. The new design was still open. [Transcript, Ambassador Program and wallet check with a player on mic](../transcription/2026-01-29.txt)

Another question concerned **Legacy Staking**. On January 22 the team had described a 45-day claim window. This time someone asked about people who would still be unable to withdraw their principal when rewards ended. Euler said there were no compensation arrangements at the time. The old program was originally supposed to pay rewards for a year and had been extended by nearly another year. His estimate was that about 80% could already be unstaked, less than 20% remained locked, and the last portion would unlock in summer. The transcript refers to both the number of stakers and the amount staked, so the exact denominator is unclear. These figures concerned the legacy contract. ## 3. A weekly Tribe Heist could fund its prize pool with entry fees and buffs

Euler said the current Heist had no clear start and finish for each competition. Its Elo rankings were vulnerable to manipulation, especially at the expense of lower-level players. The team drafted a **Tribe vs. Tribe** mode with something at stake. It would keep the main combat and scoring mechanics while making Tribes the competitors.

While proposing a weekly Tribe competition to address manipulation of Heist rankings, Euler explained how the purchase of buffs would grow the prize pool.

> **Euler**
>
> “As you purchase these modifiers, your Z's, your shaman, uh, you get like many different advantages, but whatever you have paid for those modifiers, uh, that is added to the total uh risk pool. So naturally, it would mean as the tribe gets more buffs, it contributes more to the pool.”

| Rule under discussion | Effect |
| --- | --- |
| Tribes pay an entry fee to compete for about a week | Each member contributes points, and Tribes compare their scores on a leaderboard. |
| Tribe members can pay for modifiers | Buffs could apply to Zee or Shamans, with the spend going into that round's prize pool. |
| Prizes are settled by Tribe rank after the week | Euler envisioned the highest-scoring Tribe winning the prize and splitting it. He did not specify how members would share it. |
| Entry and modifiers would use $VEE | Jackal suggested that lower-tier buffs might eventually take Coins. That was a further idea. |

The design was open for feedback. The team especially wanted to know whether players would take the risk of paying to enter and would organize a team for weekly Tribe competition. Entry fees, team sizes, matchmaking, ways to prevent manipulation, and prize formulas were all open. If buffs directly affect combat strength, Tribes that can afford them could grow the prize pool while raising the bar to compete for ordinary Tribes. [Heist documentation, for the existing mode name](https://whitepaper.zee-verse.com/gameplay/pvp/heist-under-construction) · [Transcript, Tribe Heist draft and closing clarification on $VEE](../transcription/2026-01-29.txt)

## 4. The public build serves as the soft-launch test, and existing players can help test the new tutorial

A player asked why the team was bringing in users from Canada and the Philippines while content was still in development and yet giving existing players no access to unfinished features. Euler described small tests through controlled channels. The team wanted **a few thousand first-time players for each major update**. Observing where they stopped playing would help locate unclear parts of the tutorial and give the team a way to interpret specific community feedback. The public build already provided an accessible test environment. Opening a frequently changing internal staging build would add maintenance. Once the tutorial was out, the hosts invited existing players to start fresh accounts, run through it, and report back. [Transcript, soft launch, internal staging, and the invitation to test onboarding](../transcription/2026-01-29.txt)

A player asked why the team was bringing in new users in Canada and the Philippines while development continued, without giving veterans test access to reportedly finished content.

> **Euler**
>
> “That means we need at least few thousand players in every update, so we can see, OK, uh, this is where like people stop playing. This is where people uh seem to run in issue or don't understand what to do next.”

Euler put the game at a few hundred daily active users at the time, occasionally approaching or reaching a thousand. The team felt this was too small to support long-term content development and aimed to bring in hundreds of thousands of new players. Those were management's figures and goals. The hosts published no retention, subsidy cost, or market sample breakdown. On why there were no dates for work that already existed, Euler said quests were being developed in parallel branches, with release timing dependent on integration and priorities. ## 10. Overall assessment

**The exit rules are the most important detail to check from this call.** Players can check their own wallets for returned Ambassador Program stakes. Unlock times and reward rules for legacy staking still need to be checked against the applicable contracts. The team acknowledged the incentive program had produced too few genuine referrals. The next test is whether rewards reach people bringing in new players or merely change how they are paid. FOREST's first competition confirms that an event ran. Leaderboard data, fees, and trading after incentives end would help show whether participation can last.

In the game, a time limit and a shared Tribe goal could give Heist players a clear reason to play together. Whether new teams want to enter depends heavily on fees, buff limits, matchmaking, and who receives the prize pool. The tutorial and Stash were nearer-term changes, and it should be easier to check whether they reduce friction. The missing showcase images, balance figures, and quest release dates are the main limits on what this call establishes.
