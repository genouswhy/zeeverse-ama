# Zeeverse AMA Report | 2026-04-16

April 16, 2026 | Community Call | Main speakers: Euler and Jackal | The speakers said about 35 minutes had passed when they moved to FOREST. The transcript has no timestamps, and the full running time is unverified.

The most concrete proposal for established players was to **reopen the existing Heist mode and let the community vote on rule changes the system can support**. The team also discussed a dedicated HRT pool and fixed-budget buybacks in place of direct exchange for energy potions. The case for trying this alongside the main quest was straightforward. Players were tired of waiting, and onboarding still took most of the development effort. The FOREST discussion covered the first Vault, Forest Spin scoring and the SDK rollout, and clarified the roles of Zeeverse and FOREST.

## I. The community could adjust Heist within the existing framework

[09:18](#t=558)

Players pointed out that most new content was for onboarding and worried the team was overlooking the existing community. The team said about 90% of its staff were still working on Zeeverse, with most development time going to onboarding and extending the main quest. Euler acknowledged that experienced players were tired of waiting and wanted a way to get the community involved without rebuilding the mode from scratch. In the old Heist, well-resourced players could repeatedly farm rewards, while lower-level players found the rules unfair. Changes also drew concerns about returns from players who had invested more. Release 1.7.3 had paused Heist for a redesign. The original mode had players attack and defend one another's vaults. [1.7.3 release notes](https://zeeverse.com/news/1-7-3-release-notes) · [Heist documentation](https://whitepaper.zee-verse.com/gameplay/pvp/heist-under-construction)

The idea was to **restore a working version of the old mode first, then adjust it every week or two**. Players could submit ideas on the Tuesday stream and in similar forums. Jackal would collect them for a vote, and the team would implement approved changes that fit the existing system. Examples included resetting rankings weekly, starting rank decay after 24 hours of inactivity with a possible effect on rewards, and fixing issues with the protection range. The relationship between rank decay and the new HRT rewards would depend on the final rules. Jackal joked about banning himself from competing. Voting eligibility and scope, launch rules and update frequency were all open. Asked about timing, Euler said a subsequent release might be possible and estimated a week or two, pending a check with the developers for blockers. Release 1.10.0, announced on April 20, was the next server update around that time and did not announce the return of Heist. [1.10.0 release notes](https://zeeverse.com/news/1-10-0-release-notes)

Players worried that established players would keep waiting while the team focused on onboarding. With no capacity to rebuild Heist, Jackal suggested letting the community adjust the existing mode.

> **Jackal** · [13:37](#t=817)
>
> “And for example, we can look to implement them weekly or bi-weekly, and that would be like, for example, uh, for example, we we come to, we go to my streams on Tuesdays, and then we can uh brainstorm. And then I put out these propositions, and people vote, and if the vote passes, we implement them.”

## II. Heist rewards could use an HRT pool, with community input on issuance and incentives

[15:31](#t=931)

The proposed economic experiment would put **Heist Reward Token (HRT) into a GEX liquidity pool**. Players could sell the HRT they earned into the pool, and direct conversion into energy potions would be removed. Euler first gave an example budget of **5 million to 10 million $VEE** a month, then illustrated a fixed allocation of **10 million $VEE** to buy back HRT through GEX. The team invited the community to weigh in on how much HRT to issue and whether rewards should be distributed daily or by season. The buyback was a proposed subsidy. The final budget, the chains available for trading and the price could not yet be established.

After illustrating a fixed buyback budget through GEX, Euler described the decisions about issuance and reward periods he would leave to the community.

> **Euler** · [16:02](#t=962)
>
> “It's up to community to decide uh how how many token, how many high tokens they want to reward uh every season or every day or or whatever. And and and they can decide to even like completely remove the high tokens.”

Direct conversion gave HRT value as a source of an everyday item. Under market settlement, issuing too much HRT in a season would dilute the support provided by the same budget. Player votes would therefore affect the effective value of rewards. Release 1.7.5 in 2025 had already listed an on-chain $HRT–$VEE pool. This call discussed routing the redesigned Heist's rewards and buybacks through GEX. Release 1.16.0 on August 12 later confirmed one change, the removal of the option to make energy potions with HRT. That release does not establish that the April buyback or reopening plan was implemented. [1.7.5 release notes](https://zeeverse.com/news/1-7-5-release-notes) · [1.16.0 release notes](https://zeeverse.com/news/1-16-0-release-notes)

## IV. The Land discussion distinguished generation, resource gameplay and in-world access

[17:40](#t=1060)

A listener asked for a date the team could be held to after the long wait for Land. The team responded strongly and invited players with questions to speak on the call or discuss them privately. Euler explained that the generator was already available. Generating personalized terrain and resource layouts in batches avoided hand-building large numbers of islands. Skills such as Woodcutting, Firemaking, Mining, Crafting, Summoning and Soulcrafting would also need to connect to Land gameplay. A working generator and accessible Land in the game world were separate deliverables. The call made no new commitment about an opening date, the status of Tribe Wars or how players would gain entry. ## V. FOREST reviewed the first Vault and Spin scoring, with the SDK next

[31:55](#t=1915)

Euler said the platform had reached a **$1 million** trading-volume milestone the night before. He gave neither the measurement window nor a breakdown. He explained that a new project token first pairs with $FOREST in a pool, so buying a project token with BNB goes through $FOREST. The call described liquidity fees remaining in the curve pool as the economic effect. It shared no transaction-level fee data, evidence of burns or price results. [FOREST economy documentation](https://forest.inc/docs/whitepaper/forest-and-seed-economy)

**This round of Forest Spin counted actual token trading volume only.** Euler directly answered whether the minigames counted and corrected the earlier description that had suggested playing could affect the leaderboard. He described it as the third trading competition, already complete, and said he had seen participation extend beyond the project's own token. The first Seed Vault had concluded. Euler estimated that it consumed **about 10–15%** of circulating supply and said he had not checked again. The official history records **8,254,756.07 SEED** consumed, 99 participants and a prize pool of 100,000 $FOREST. The share of circulating supply remains an estimate from the call. ## VI. Closing questions on acquisition, the two projects and feedback from established players

[41:04](#t=2464)

- **The next wave of players.** Players in the previous round progressed through the first main quest faster than expected. The team planned a larger sample for the next round to see whether there was enough content for the first day and the first few days. The call gave no new budget, sample size or retention results.
- **The direction four years out.** Euler described Zeeverse as an MMORPG the team wanted to operate for years. He used RuneScape's long buildup of content and its player economy to explain how long it takes to build depth. FOREST's goal was to make technology built for Zeeverse available to outside creators as tools and a distribution platform. The Zeeverse team could keep improving the game while FOREST took on marketing.
- **Questions about the motives of NFT and token projects.** The hosts asked players to judge what had shipped from the release records and said development would continue. They shared no financial breakdown, guaranteed returns or new distribution progress. Players could file tickets for wild Zee battle bugs. The remark about the battle screen being upside down was made in jest.
- **Giveaway claims.** Jackal drew three one-month Premium Passes from the attendee list at the end. Winners could open a ticket to receive a code.

## Overall assessment

This was one of the calls that turned frustration among established players into a concrete way to participate. Heist can be judged by how broadly players at different levels take part in votes, whether they can find opponents after rule changes, and whether anyone can still profit from farming rewards repeatedly. A dedicated HRT pool will depend on effective rewards for ordinary participants and the pool's ability to absorb seasonal issuance. For the new quest bosses, a first defeat should show players enough to work out the mechanics. That will help determine whether the same dungeons can challenge veterans and still keep newcomers playing.
