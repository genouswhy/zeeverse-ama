# Zeeverse / FOREST AMA Deep Dive | Chapter 3 is live, and the map, Zee, and gathering are changing together

November 5, 2026 | Community Call | Main speakers identified by how they addressed each other: Euler and Jackal

With Chapter 3 live, Zeeverse has begun tackling a longer-term question. Can players pick the right Zee for what they want to do today, travel to a distinctive area, and make progress by gathering, fighting, and crafting? Jackal previewed gathering and crafting passives for follower Zee, eggs matching wild Zee, and six new tools. Euler talked through the map plans, including themes and recognizable roads, clusters of the same resources, more Stash access, and wild Zee that attack first. FOREST's Windows version was available, and the team was using the Harness to make player acquisition material for Zeeverse. [Full archived transcript](../transcription/2026-11-05.txt)

## 1. Chapter 3 is live, and the legendary ring can give one separate loot roll

[01:51](#t=111)

Jackal opened by saying recent quests had received positive feedback. A few issues showed up in production at launch and, according to him, the team had handled them. Work had begun on several more quests and the campaign finale. The **November 2** notes for 1.18.0 list Chapter 3, **Third Time’s the Charm**, the third Totem, **The Ship**, **The Wicker**, and 20 digging locations. The **November 4** notes for 1.18.1 list fixes involving entry to Umbra, land plot interactions, village pathfinding, and visuals in The Wicker. [Zeeverse 1.18.0 release notes](https://zeeverse.com/news/1-18-0-release-notes) · [Zeeverse 1.18.1 release notes](https://zeeverse.com/news/1-18-1-release-notes)

Jackal said treasure map chests held the only legendary ring at the time. It gives a **20% chance to roll the loot table again**. In his example, a player finishes a level of Umbra and rolls a **Judicator’s Staff**, then might get another on the second roll if lucky. The second roll only happens if the perk triggers. The loot table still determines what drops. Near the close, a player asked why getting the same “card” again had not produced a chest. Jackal said treasure hunting also had a **5% chance to award an additional Treasure Map**. Both numbers came from this call. The former is the chance that a ring wearer gets another roll, and the latter is a bonus map reward. Jackal did not repeat the odds of getting the ring from a chest. ## 2. Zee passives depend on your current follower, and evolution can unlock a second one

[02:52](#t=172)

Jackal said the next update would add **Zee Passives**, misheard as “Z Passports” at the start of the transcript. A logging passive could award an extra log, for example, with the effect improving as players trained their Zee. Other passives could apply to mining, fishing, and cooking. The call described one passive for every eligible Zee and a second after evolution, with mountable Zee proposed as an exception. The current **Active Zee** provides the effect. The team wanted players to switch followers to match the activity and have a reason to train companions outside their combat lineup.

After listing woodcutting, mining, fishing, and cooking, Jackal used logs to show what a follower passive could do. He went on to explain that using only the Active Zee’s perk encouraged players to switch and train different Zee.

> **Jackal** · [02:52](#t=172)
>
> “And every, when you have like for example woodcutting trade, it will give you a chance to give you an extra log when you, um, cut down a tree. Now this chance will grow as you level it up.”

**After the call**, the November 10 notes for 1.18.2 described the shipped **follower Zee** and **skill perks**. Most Zee have one perk and get a second on evolution. Trigger probability rises every ten levels and can reach **25%** at level 99. Rewards include extra wood, ore, or fish, cooking two fish at once, double Souls, or saving seeds, wood, ingredients, and spell costs. Extra items still grant skill XP. The 25% figure comes from the later notes. The call gave no odds. [1.18.2 release notes, published November 10 after the call](https://zeeverse.com/news/1-18-2-release-notes) · [Zeeverse Skills documentation](https://whitepaper.zee-verse.com/gameplay/skills)

## 3. Wild Zee would drop their own eggs, with existing Lobster supply rules holding

[03:46](#t=226)

Jackal said the team wanted wild Zee to have a small chance of dropping an egg of the same species. A player who wants a particular Zee could target wild encounters with that species. That would let players seek out the companions they wanted, especially if they had yet to collect all the starters. He specifically said **the Lobster Egg would stay out of these drops because its supply was locked**. The call gave no species-level odds or trading rules.

Monk asked how the team would handle an expanding Zee supply. Euler said the team had a proposal that might be announced early the following week. He wanted the new way to acquire Zee to be more meaningful. The call gave no details or exact announcement date. Afterward, the 1.18.2 notes confirmed that defeating wild Zee could drop an egg of the same species and that bosses also drop eggs. Existing Fire, Water, and Grass eggs became Zee eggs of the corresponding element. The same release removed the Zee page from the Marketplace and returned listed Zee to their holders. The notes did not present these changes as the full supply proposal discussed on the call. The next two tiers would be stronger. For materials, the call said Elder Logs could already be bought on GEX. The team also wanted to add Frost and Coral wood to Umbra. Players would have to reach the required Crafting levels and gather ingredients. The team wanted resources already produced by Land to remain useful. ## 5. Areas, roads, and resource sites should help players navigate without opening the minimap

[12:19](#t=739)

Euler said the map's overall layout had changed little in years. Many buildings were empty. Some mountaintop buildings, GEX, and Totems differed in art style, and complex vegetation looked cluttered as voxels. The team had started a wider art overhaul and was using an editor to reposition objects from the in-game view. The November 2 release added **area names** and notifications on entry. The team wanted each area to have its own plants, materials, and landmarks, so players could recognize it on sight, as they already could at Umbra. The main roads, side roads, and hidden paths would look different. Main roads would clearly lead to the next area, a port, or a town. [1.18.0 release notes on area names](https://zeeverse.com/news/1-18-0-release-notes) · [Call transcript on reworking the map](../transcription/2026-11-05.txt)

Euler said different kinds of roads looked much the same on the existing map. Recognizable main roads would orient players while leaving other routes open for exploration.

> **Euler** · [12:19](#t=739)
>
> “So the main road will be very distinguishable from like these smaller side roads. And that means whenever you are, uh, on the main road, you always know that just by keep going, by keeping going in that direction, you will find yourself, uh, entering the next region or or next next harbor or city.”

The area layout would change three parts of the experience.

- **Resources clustered together.** Players would not have to visit five places to find one kind of high-tier tree. Several gathering nodes could sit together at a few key locations, with some resources unique to particular areas. The team thought this would make it easier to meet players doing the same thing and to plan yields and repeat routes.
- **Supplies and services along the way.** The team wanted a few small outposts, with Stash access in **about three places**, and possibly entrances to services such as Hoarder and Sanctuary. Players could store items or heal nearby after fighting or gathering. The number and locations were estimates. Jackal noted that moving locations could also move some quest objectives.
- **Some wild Zee would attack first.** Near mines or wooded areas, certain Zee might start battles. Low-level Shamans might be blocked. Players who could handle the area could gather and train in combat in the same place. Certain Zee might also drop crafting materials. Once areas were easier to recognize, Euler hoped players could discover **around 9–10 new Zee** there and keep collecting after finding all the starters. The call gave no lineup, spawn rules, or attack range. [Call transcript on resource sites, Stash access, and aggressive wild Zee](../transcription/2026-11-05.txt)

Someone asked about a day-night cycle. Euler said it was outside the current plans. Making quest characters and resource respawns follow a cycle would affect many systems. The more practical option was using distinctive lighting to make some places feel like nighttime. The team showed a work-in-progress screenshot. Jackal described a small-scale refresh and asked people familiar with the old gameplay to keep sending feedback. Based on the earlier discussion about bringing it back, he may have meant Heist. The transcript does not identify the screenshot. The map would probably be **rearranged in stages and polished afterward**. Euler estimated the world boss and map work would not be too far apart. The call gave no exact date for the Great Tree. [Call transcript on the map Q&A and release timing](../transcription/2026-11-05.txt)

He planned to reduce its power slightly and rework its Ability. He also proposed weakening **Nina Wach’a**. The call gave no numbers or full list. ## 10. My overall take

The map and Zee passives make more sense together when you look at how they'll affect everyday play. A player gathering wood could pick a Zee with a logging perk, take the main road to a cluster of high-tier trees, watch for wild Zee that attack first, and stop at a nearby Stash afterward. The companion and route each have a practical effect. If grouping resources only means more queues and competition for nodes, the area will become somewhere players simply commute to. How many players the high-tier sites can support and how long they stay are worth watching.

The new eggs, wood, and tools start to form a fuller progression path. New players can target a specific Zee, veterans have a reason to train non-combat companions, and gathering and crafting can give each other purpose. Whether that holds up depends on whether strong passives make a few Zee and routes the only worthwhile options, and whether Land resources still have clear demand. FOREST has a similarly concrete test. Can Zeeverse actually run ads made with the Harness and try out more ways to play in the same production time?
