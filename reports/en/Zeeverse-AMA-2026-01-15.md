# Zeeverse / FOREST AMA Deep Dive | January 15, 2026

January 15, 2026 · Community Call · Jackal opened the call, Euler joined later, and players came on mic · Toward the end, the hosts said the call had been running for about 70 minutes

The most concrete gameplay change discussed was to **make a first dungeon clear feel like an expedition while keeping the familiar battle entry for repeat runs**. The two stages serve different needs. New players need to understand why they are fighting within the first half hour. Veteran players grinding toward level 99 still need efficient daily runs. The hosts also discussed Great Tree, the World Boss, and The Forge in terms of quest progression after players asked why content that was reportedly close to complete had yet to open. Euler addressed the theft of FOREST staking rewards, the delayed December airdrop, and the team's financial runway.

Speaker attribution follows arrivals, handoffs, and how speakers address one another. Version details use release notes that were public at the time. ## 2. Great Tree opens through a quest, and players fight together for a World Boss drop

[04:53](#t=293)

Jackal showed an earlier blockout of Great Tree. There were entrances to explore beneath the tree and two kinds of creatures along the quest route. Players would work their way from smaller creatures to a quest boss they had to defeat. Completing the quest would give access to the World Boss, which would appear as a wild Zee. Jackal also shared animations for a small spider-like creature. The quest map and cinematics were still being integrated.

The World Boss design kept the **timed turns** of Zee combat. Players would participate in the same encounter on synchronized turns, and the boss would attack everyone. The team had discussed letting it focus its attack on a single player and was concerned that might be too punishing. Another key detail was that **only one person would receive the drop being discussed on each kill**. Jackal expected the boss to be difficult to solo. Without server selection, players would run into one another, and a Tribe could organize a kill and split the loot themselves. Drop eligibility, how the recipient would be decided, how encounters would begin or reset, and any automatic loot distribution were all open. ## 4. First dungeon clears would add exploration and choices, with direct stage selection for repeats

[14:05](#t=845)

Jackal proposed making dungeon environments walkable on at least the first clear. Turn-based Zee combat would remain central. After a battle, players could explore side paths, collect loot, or solve puzzles. They might have to mine a rock blocking the way or catch and cook a fish at a campfire. Euler gave a practical example. Before making a detour to fight an optional boss, players would need to consider how much strength their team had left. A fishing spot could let them cook food and recover before moving on. A first trip through The Forge could then begin at the entrance, ask players to make choices along the way, and culminate in an encounter with **Vulcan** inside the dungeon.

**First clears and regular repeat runs would move at different speeds.** Jackal said players could use the existing stage selection and replay battles after the first clear, preserving access for training and autobattles. The team would use its AI World Builder to accelerate map creation and design fixed environments for each first clear. Umbra and The Forge could gradually adopt that structure. The expectation at the time was that Hollow Trials would largely retain its existing format. Euler said the team could use more of the generation tool's capabilities internally and add custom designs. He illustrated the production potential by saying it could generate a land area similar in scale to the existing world in a few hours. He gave no timings for finishing or testing it. [Official Land Generator introduction, for tool context](https://zeeverse.com/news/zeeverse-worlds-from-words) · [Transcript, dungeon redesign and Euler's optional boss example](../transcription/2026-01-15.txt)

## 5. The first half hour should give players clear goals and faster early progression

[52:46](#t=3166)

A player on mic asked whether the amount of content already in Zeeverse made the game too difficult to pick up. Euler acknowledged that the game offered little direction and that progression was too flat. A new player and someone who had played for months felt nearly the same rate of progress. The team wanted to introduce systems in smaller steps through quests and dungeons. A first dungeon clear could award more early XP to help a Zee gain a few levels quickly, and quests could give more skill XP so players saw the value of each step. **Returning to the normal progression rate after about a week or two** was Euler's example. The XP values and number of days were still open.

A player suggested letting newcomers try high-level equipment or Zee before sending them back to the start. Euler said that could fit into the early story. The existing tutorial already let players briefly use a level 99 monkey-like Zee, and he felt the sequence could do more to convey its power. An advanced encounter only makes an impression if players can understand the moves and see what is happening. The goal was for all new players to understand the controls, what they were doing, and why within the first 30 minutes. Players coming from Web2 and Web3 faced similar hurdles. The team wanted better retention before expanding marketing or working with more influencers. The tools and infrastructure built in 2024 and 2025 were intended to support later content releases.

Euler encouraged the community to run polls and rally support so the team could see which ideas mattered most. The team would assess whether it could use a proposal in whole or in part. Jackal said suggestions such as new ways to spend in-game Coins would be scheduled according to urgency, expected benefit, and complexity. A straightforward change might make the next minor release. The community asked for more development screenshots, and both hosts were open to showing work in progress while avoiding spoilers. ## 8. FOREST addresses the staking reward theft and weighs a SEED auction for future airdrops

[1:06:27](#t=3987)

A player on mic asked for the transaction hashes and addresses associated with the stolen funds. Euler said the team and its lawyers had reported the incident through the relevant channels and that the community was tracking the funds. He estimated that about **1.5 million $FOREST** had left the former staking rewards contract, representing a loss of about **$100,000**. He said the funds later went through a mixer. Euler considered the chances of recovery slim. The call included no specific hashes or wallet addresses. He said the team had reviewed its AWS infrastructure and dependencies and encrypted data that had previously been unencrypted. This was the team's account of its response, without on-chain evidence or an independent security audit presented on the call.

The same player asked whether the team had enough funding for another year of development. Euler estimated that the loss was less than a month of the team's operating costs and that its runway was close to **two years** at the time. The team said it did not rely on token sales for everyday expenses. The estimate came from management, and no cost breakdown or financial statements were disclosed.

Later, another player asked about **the portion of December's token airdrop that had yet to be distributed**. The player noted that some recipients had staked for a year and that the delay also put pressure on long-term holders. Euler acknowledged that some staked and others sold. The team wanted the airdrop to reward early players and bring attention to the protocol, so it needed a mechanism for distribution. It discussed **trying a Launchpad trading competition, building a way to bid SEED points for airdrops, and testing the auction at a smaller scale**. The plan published in November 2025 described splitting a fixed prize pool in proportion to the points spent. This call gave no date, eligibility criteria, or allocations for the delayed portion. ## 11. Overall assessment

**This proposal accounts for both the first clear and the time spent on repeat runs.** Route choices, optional dangers, and supplies picked up along the way give new players a reason to pay attention to the dungeon itself. Preserving stage selection after the first clear would let existing players keep their training routines. A full first-clear route, the number of actions needed to start a repeat run, and completion rates for the tutorial and first dungeon would give us the best evidence of how it works.

The main unresolved question about the World Boss is **how a player's contribution translates into rewards**. A whole group participates, yet only one member gets the drop. Tribes might agree on how to split the proceeds, while players who join on the spot might find the payoff unpredictable. Whether waiting for the encounter, healing, dealing damage, or landing the final hit affects eligibility will matter more to participation than the size of the boss. FOREST airdrops raise a similar concern for existing holders, who need a distribution schedule and rules they can verify. The call set out the design direction. Reward formulas, official release dates, on-chain details of the incident, and financial documentation remain outstanding.
