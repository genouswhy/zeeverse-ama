# Zeeverse / FOREST AMA In-Depth Report | May 28, 2026

May 28, 2026 | Community Call | Main speakers Euler and Jackal | [English transcript](../transcription/2026-05-28.txt)

The biggest news for players was **how the third part of the main quest would connect dungeons, life skills, fast travel, and treasure maps**. The team previewed a third Metal Totem, two new dungeons, and equipment slots for rings and necklaces. The story introduces the first Treasure Map. Later maps would turn dungeon runs into repeatable treasure hunts across the world.

Paid user acquisition had started in the Philippines, giving the team its first data on tutorial completion and performance on low-end Android devices. Plans for bringing back Heist also became more specific. The team said **HRT would be removed from energy potion recipes and made tradable on GEX**, with an off-chain pool as the priority. FOREST previewed a Builder that generates and deploys on-chain games from prompts, with plans to charge $FOREST for credits used to make model calls. [May 20 FOREST roadmap announcement](https://forest.inc/news/forest-protocol-agentic-economy)

## 3. The first Treasure Map teaches digging, and players can hold only one later map at a time

[26:36](#t=1596)

Clearing the ship dungeon gives players their first **Treasure Map**. Joran lends or provides a **Spade**. Players follow the map, dig up a **Gold Ring** at the marked spot, and learn how to use an equipment slot. The third main quest is set to introduce **two slots, one for a ring and one for a necklace**. The first standard accessories were described as comparable in strength to iron-tier gear. The team wanted Land owners to craft and enchant higher-tier rings with combat stats, adding another product to the future Land economy. Jackal also linked accessories to Mr. Block's chest and left its exact reward pool for later discussion.

Regular Treasure Maps were planned as rare dungeon drops. Wild Zees came up as another possible source. Each location comes from a set of predefined spots, so experienced players may learn to find their way from memory. The first story chest has a fixed drop. Jackal gave an approximate **1 in 5,000** target for finding a **Legendary ring** in later rare chests. Euler said the rate could still change. The figure describes opening the chest after obtaining and using a rare map. The odds for an individual dungeon run were not given. The ring has a special effect outside combat. The team might reveal it before launch or let the first player to find it discover what it does.

Treasure Maps were intended to be untradeable. Once a player has one, they need to finish it before another can drop. The Casket found by digging can be recycled and was also described as untradeable. Jackal imagined players who find the rare ring being able to keep or sell it. The design makes players complete individual maps and do the digging, reducing the incentive to use many alternate accounts or stockpile maps through dungeon runs alone. The call did not give the drop rate for standard maps, what players receive for recycling a Casket, or the final trading rules.

A player joked about creating a huge number of accounts. Jackal used that to explain the one-map limit.

> **Jackal** · [30:48](#t=1848)
>
> “You can only have one treasure map at a time, so you won't be able to A F K Umbra for, uh, 500, uh, dungeons, and get like twenty treasure maps, and then do them in one go. \[…\] And if you don't do that, and do more dungeons, you won't be able to get any more treasure map drops.”

## 5. FOREST covers Seeds spent in auctions, protocol fee retention, and a Builder demo

[00:55](#t=55)

### Auctions consume Seeds, and Euler says $FOREST protocol fees go to cold storage

[01:09](#t=69)

Someone asked whether the supply of Seeds could grow indefinitely. Euler said points can keep accruing in theory, with issuance controlled in practice by the activities that earn points and by trading volume. As an example, he said the previous trading competition distributed about **500,000 Seeds**, while the last Genesis Vault consumed around **9 million Seeds**. The official site records that Genesis #3 consumed about **9.67 million Seeds** at settlement. Auctions consume the Seeds committed to bids, and participation in each round affects the reward per eligible Seed. [Official Seed Vault rules](https://forest.inc/news/seed-vaults-the-economy-behind-the-forest-protocol-flywheel) [Seed Vault settlement page](https://forest.inc/vault)

Euler described a different process for $FOREST. FOREST provides the protocol's underlying liquidity and routes trades. He said protocol fees from those trades were pooled and kept in a cold wallet. Participants asked whether a previous security incident could affect those holdings. Euler said the wallet involved in that incident was a hot wallet used for staking rewards and that its keys were exposed through a third-party dependency. This was the team's verbal account of custody. The call showed no cold wallet address, permissions, or incident report. The official documentation covers **Seeds consumed in auctions** and **FOREST protocol fees, buybacks, and retention** as distinct mechanisms. [FOREST and Seeds documentation](https://forest.inc/docs/whitepaper/forest-and-seed-economy)

### Builder brings prompts, token configuration, and SDK integration into one tool

[06:01](#t=361)

The team described three steps. Early templates such as coin flips helped test the components. **Playkit**, announced on May 23, already provided the SDK, settlement, and an Agent Skill. The next step shown on the call was a prompt interface for creators. The tool would generate an app, handle SDK integration and token economy configuration, then deploy it on BNB Chain. Euler joked that even a three-year-old could use it, stressing that the intended audience includes people who do not program. [May 20 Forest Builder roadmap](https://forest.inc/news/forest-protocol-agentic-economy) [May 23 Playkit announcement](https://forest.inc/news/forest-protocol-the-settlemeny-layer-for-on-chain-games)

Generating an app incurs model usage costs, and the team wanted creators to **exchange $FOREST for usage credits**. Euler said a simple app cost about **2–3 $FOREST** in internal tests. The cost of complex projects and formal pricing were still being worked out. He suggested there might be product and event news the following week, with no details announced. **Post-call verification.** FOREST announced Forest Builder on June 15 and described exchanging $FOREST for Builder Credits, then reviewing, configuring, and deploying the generated app. [June 15 Forest Builder announcement](https://forest.inc/news/forest-builder-imagine-describe-deploy-on-chain)

After introducing prompt-based creation, Euler explained how usage credits would work and shared an internal estimate for a small test app.

> **Euler** · [08:37](#t=517)
>
> “So essentially users will be using Forest to only pay for the credits they're using when when they're building these apps, launching them. Um, we're still like in a works, tweak in a bit, but right now, from our latest tests, it's probably around two, three, uh, for the tokens to launch like a really simple mini app.”

## 8. My overall assessment

**Treasure Maps could turn a dungeon drop into an adventure of its own.** A player gets a clue to a destination after clearing a dungeon, heads out into the world with a spade, and brings the finds back to equip or use. The limit of one map at a time gives players a reason to finish each hunt. A fixed story reward teaches everyone to use the system, and the extremely rare ring gives dedicated treasure hunters a longer-term goal. For this to be worth repeating, the ordinary Casket drops will need to make the trip worthwhile on their own.

The first data from the Philippines lets the team test **the experience on low-end devices and how far players get through quests** at the same time. Worth watching are the scope of the sample of more than 400 players, where people drop out after the tutorial, whether the off-chain Heist pool opens and how much liquidity it receives, and how many credits Builder projects need to go from simple apps to games that can keep running.
