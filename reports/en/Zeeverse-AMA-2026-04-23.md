# Zeeverse / FOREST AMA In-Depth Report | April 23, 2026

April 23, 2026 | Community Call | Main speakers Euler and Jackal | [English transcript](../transcription/2026-04-23.txt)

Players recruited through the latest round of paid acquisition got through Zeeverse’s early campaign faster than the team expected. Euler said **about 40% of the players in that sample completed the quest on their first day**. Many reached the end of the available story without a clear next step. The team paused acquisition to finish the second part of the campaign and give players more to do alongside the daily login rewards and overworld exploration rewards already in the game. The plan was then to run a larger test.

For longtime players, the biggest discussion was **how to bring back Heist and how $VEE would fund its rewards**. FOREST was testing a different approach. Seed Vault ties together staking, trading, and event points, while the Stack competition and forthcoming SDK give the team ways to test what players can do with tokens inside a game.

## 1. Paid acquisition exposed a gap in the campaign, with retention still to measure

Euler said the team recruits a batch of players once a build is reasonably stable, then uses the data to assess the design and player flows before deciding how to run the next round. It had already begun A/B testing Google Play store graphics to see which images attracted more players. The latest batch advanced through the campaign faster than expected. The **nearly 40%** figure was the share of that sample who completed the quest on day one. Long-term retention, growth in daily active users, and how many people stayed after the campaign were still open questions. The team paused paid acquisition that week while it prepared new marketing assets and more content.

Asked about retention and daily activity after acquisition, Euler explained why the team had paused ads that week.

> **Euler**
>
> “And once they complete the quest, they still find themselves without, um, without the purpose, so generally without, uh, the next campaign. So, but overall we saw, I, I think, close to like 40 percent of, uh, day one completion for the quests.”

The **1.10.0** update on April 20 added Daily Rewards, giving new players a reason to return the next day. Berries, mushrooms, apples, and light wood for campfires began appearing in the world. Each player can discover each overworld chest once. The patch also refreshed the Hatchery’s look and animations, added Zee experience to the reward screen, and let players choose whether the chat bar starts open or hidden. Jackal said the team wanted to add small puzzles and fights to some one-time chests, with better prizes than the early chests. The specifics would come in later updates. [April 20 release notes for 1.10.0](https://zeeverse.com/news/1-10-0-release-notes)

The roughly 40% first-day completion rate tells us how many players the available quest carried to its endpoint. Once the team adds more content, it can look at where players get stuck and what they do when they come back the next day. Those results will help answer the questions the earlier test left open.

## 2. Campaign part two was estimated at two to three weeks, with Jungle access tied to completion

Jackal said this part would continue the story of what Aka set in motion. It would add **two dungeons, two bosses, Rako, a raccoon-like Dark Matter Zee**, and new NPCs to move the quest forward. His estimate was **two to three weeks** to public release. Longtime players with established teams were expected to clear it quickly. Its main role was to teach newcomers how to advance the story and take on dungeons. **After the call**, the May 11 announcement for 1.11.0 listed the new main quest **A Longer Ask**, changes to early dungeons, new bosses, and Rako. [May 11 release notes for 1.11.0](https://zeeverse.com/news/1-11-0-release-notes)

Jackal said that after the update, players would need to complete the main quest to regain access to the Jungle. They would repair a second Totem near the Sawmill and unlock fast travel between the two Totems. That route could shorten the walk for regular Hollow Trials players. The team also discussed bringing players closer to the cave through a future Great Tree teleport or putting a teleport ability on jewelry. Jackal used Mr. Block’s jewelry as an example. The item and destination were still undecided.

After previewing the new dungeons in campaign part two, Jackal gave a release estimate and explained the requirement for re-entering the Jungle.

> **Jackal**
>
> “Not this week, and I'm pretty sure not next week either. We're looking at like two to three weeks, until we are ready to, uh, release that into the public. And, uh, with this update, uh, you will be losing the ability to access the Jungle area until you cle- clear the campaign.”

## 3. The plan was to bring Heist back with five Zees and let the community vote on the rules

Heist had been suspended in an earlier release. Jackal said a revised version tried by some Tribe players received mixed reviews, and the team needed to prioritize the early campaign. His plan at the time was to bring Heist back with **five Zees**, then turn community proposals into questions players could vote on. Examples included **whether to reset Elo each week and whether to award more points for the same amount of time**. Votes could run every two weeks or whenever enough people were available to take part. Euler was open to letting the community discuss putting Heist on-chain if the team could support it with its existing development capacity. The team reserved room to rebalance rules that gave some players too large an advantage. Jackal said a severe imbalance the team could not fix might lead to another suspension. [October 22, 2025 release notes for 1.7.3](https://zeeverse.com/news/1-7-3-release-notes)

For rewards, the team was willing to allocate some **$VEE** each month to **the Heist Token liquidity pool on GEX**. Players would earn Heist Tokens under community-approved rules and exchange them for $VEE on the pool’s terms. Who would receive the rewards, when they would get them, and whether liquidity would go in all at once or in installments were all open for discussion. A single monthly injection of ten million was an example for discussing the schedule. The actual allocation and buyback frequency were still open. GEX documentation explains that individual items trade against $VEE in separate pools, with exchange prices depending on each pool’s price and liquidity. [Official GEX documentation](https://whitepaper.zee-verse.com/economy/game-exchange-gex)

A player asked whether Heist would again offer VEE rewards. Euler explained how the team would fund the GEX pool and what decisions the community would make.

> **Euler**
>
> “So, what we said we are willing to, uh, allocate certain amount of the token every month, uh, that can be distributed in rewards. \[…\] And all we will do is provide liquidity, so those high tokens can be exchanged. But yeah, everything else is up to community to decide how, if and when.”

## 4. The trading economy depends on player progression, with mobile payments and evolution still to resolve

Asked how established players might benefit from the items they already hold, Euler used the **Shield of Souls** as an example. Once new players progress far enough to need stronger equipment, they could buy it from established players on the planned in-game marketplace, with the platform taking a commission. The example of upgrading from steel, a listing price of about **15 million $VEE**, and tens of thousands of new players illustrated how demand could arise. The call provided no actual sale price, acquisition volume, or platform commission data.

Euler hoped to attract mainstream mobile players through Facebook, TikTok, and Instagram by showing them the characters and gameplay. He saw promise in the United States and English-language markets and mentioned some barriers to entering South Korea’s domestic games market. Jackal drew on his experience of *Whiteout Survival* to explain why players pay for progression and in-game social experiences. The discussion of mobile gaming, regional spending, and willingness to pay reflected the team’s acquisition assumptions. Zeeverse’s own paid conversion still needed to be tested through the campaign results.

**Mobile in-app purchases.** The team wanted players to buy what the speakers called “gold” in the app and use it to buy items through systems such as GEX. Euler noted that an in-app purchase could be refunded after its on-chain transaction had settled, so payment and delivery needed to account for both states. When Jackal asked about Zee evolution, Euler realized that the current process meant moving a Zee on-chain first, then suggested exploring off-chain evolution. The purchase flow, evolution flow, and schedule were all open. Official documentation treats the Coins spent in routine play separately from on-chain $VEE used in systems such as evolution and GEX liquidity. The proposed in-app gold product still needed formal rules. [Official documentation on $VEE and Coins](https://whitepaper.zee-verse.com/economy/utility-tokens/usdvee)

**Why explore when there’s GEX?** Players worried that if everything had a market, people would stop looking for resources themselves. Euler wanted gatherable items to help people get started. A new player with an empty balance could find apples, sell them for their first $VEE, and buy a piece of equipment they needed. His example of getting 50 $VEE for 10 apples illustrated how that could work. The team expected GEX to take some learning for newcomers, who might spend their first days exploring the map and collecting items. [Official GEX documentation](https://whitepaper.zee-verse.com/economy/game-exchange-gex)

A player asked whether trading every item would take away the reason to explore. Euler offered a hypothetical first trade for a newcomer with an empty balance.

> **Euler**
>
> “The idea behind the GEX is that every item is tradable, and and and having some liquidity there becomes a way for people to get their first tokens. So, and and then maybe, uh, you will see people looking for apples, like picking up ten apples, uh, getting their first \[…\] 50V tokens, and then spending them to buy maybe something they actually need.”

## 5. Seed Vault connects participation, and Stack tests whether a game can hold players’ attention

Looking back on the first auction, Euler estimated that about **9 million Seeds** had been entered. He said the second auction had nearly two days to run and had drawn about **1.6 million Seeds** so far. He expected some late bids, so the reward per effective Seed would become clear only when bidding ended. The public auction record shows that bidding for Genesis #1 ran **April 4–11** and consumed **8,254,756.07 Seeds**. Genesis #2 ran **April 18–25** and consumed **4,215,305.27 Seeds**. The dates are in UTC, and April 4 and 18 on the public listing are the opening dates. Each auction had a **100,000 FOREST** prize pool. [Official Seed Vault auction record](https://forest.inc/vault)

Seed Vault allocates a fixed pool in proportion to effective Seeds, with possible multipliers for the top bidders. The general rules published on April 1 use the top ten by deposit as an example. The settings vary by auction. In the first round, the top **25** received a multiplier of up to **2.5×**. In the second, the top **10** received a multiplier. [April 1 Seed Vault rules announcement](https://forest.inc/news/seed-vaults-the-economy-behind-the-forest-protocol-flywheel) [Official Seed Vault auction record](https://forest.inc/vault)

Trading, playing games, and taking part in events can all earn Seeds alongside staking. Euler said the protocol retains fees generated by token trades. He described a cycle in which buybacks, protocol liquidity, and subsequent trading activity could reinforce one another. That was his assessment of the mechanism and the market. The call offered no transaction-level breakdown of where the fees went or what drove the market activity. [Official documentation on FOREST and Seeds](https://forest.inc/docs/whitepaper/forest-and-seed-economy)

The forthcoming **Forest SDK** was the other part of the plan, aimed at developers. Euler envisioned an SDK and companion Agent Skill that would handle deposits, withdrawals, and token integration so creators could focus on gameplay. He used a simulation where players spend tokens to upgrade mining rigs as an example. The scope of balance management and settlement depended on what each version delivered. **After the call**, FOREST released the first SDK on April 30. Its announcement listed HTML5 game integration, connected wallet details, in-game swaps, real-time quotes, and balance reads. [April 30 Forest SDK release announcement](https://forest.inc/news/forest-sdk-infra)

While describing the upcoming Forest SDK, Euler used a game example to show how tokens could be used for upgrades and production.

> **Euler**
>
> “You can basically build like a bitcoin mining simulator where people are using the coins to upgrade the their, uh, rigs. And, uh, and and then they can just, uh, uh, use it to mine more coins.”

**Stack**, from partner **Acid Gamez**, was the live example covered on the call. Players could start for free and try to score by timing their block placements. The same day’s announcement advertised **$10,000** in $STACK across multiple seasons and **$1,000** in $STACK for the first season, which ran from April 23 to May 7. [April 23 Stack event announcement](https://forest.inc/news/stack-10-000-play-campaign-by-acid-gamez)

## 6. Other questions and closing remarks

- **Does combat feel slower at 3× speed?** Jackal said the redesign mainly sped up attack actions, whereas the old design sped up the entire sequence. He planned to compare it with his earlier records of average dungeon completion times. The call included no measured results yet.
- **Could Hollow Trials get a countdown or a direct entrance?** The team preferred to keep the interface simple. It discussed a countdown at the cave entrance and checking who receives reset notifications. A direct route tied to one Zee would serve a small group, so the discussion favored access everyone could use. A one-use portal out of Hollow Cave was already available after claiming rewards. **After the call**, 1.12.1 added a cooldown timer above the cave entrance on May 22. [February 16 release notes for 1.7.8](https://zeeverse.com/news/1-7-8-release-notes) [May 22 release notes for 1.12.1](https://zeeverse.com/news/1-12-1-release-notes)
- **Will there be a raffle for Mr. Block’s chest?** Euler said the team could consider it when the chest goes live. The number of prizes and how to claim them were undecided.
- **When will Binance Land be updated?** Jackal said completing **Just One More Thing** unlocks this public Land. The team was reworking the bath pools so a soak could grant a temporary buff. He jokingly called them “liquidity pools.” Players reported that the password was still wrong. Jackal agreed to check for a block at the entrance, and the call ended without confirmation of a fix. Euler also joked about a Binance listing. [Official quest documentation for the quest name](https://whitepaper.zee-verse.com/gameplay/quests)
- **Low graphics settings and Rako’s design.** The team was improving lighting on low-resolution devices. Jackal shared an early concept for the new Umbra Zee. The discussion also included unapproved concept art, with the final design to be seen in the released update.
- **Closing raffle.** Jackal gave away **three one-month Premium Passes**. Winners could open a Discord ticket to get a code from Cherry. He said he would miss next Tuesday’s Zeeverse stream and the next Community Call while on holiday. The recording does not establish the exact spelling of all the winners’ usernames. [Official Premium Pass page](https://zeeverse.com/app/premium)

## 8. My overall assessment

The common theme was **giving players a next step once they finish the first one**. Later dungeons, rewards for returning, and discoveries around the map can give newcomers something to do after the early quests. For longtime players looking for competition, Heist would return with a playable set of rules and votes to settle disagreements. FOREST needs to see whether people return to play or use the protocol after earning Seeds, bidding them, and claiming prizes. Player behavior will tell us how well each of those steps works.

I’ll be watching **next-day retention and quest drop-off after the new campaign, Heist’s first vote and the liquidity actually added, and how many Stack and Seed Vault prize recipients come back**.
