# Zeeverse / FOREST AMA In-Depth Report | May 7, 2026

May 7, 2026 | Community Call | Main speakers Euler and Jackal | [English transcript](../transcription/2026-05-07.txt)

Zeeverse set a specific target for its next user acquisition test. Once all three early main quests are in place, the team plans to increase recruitment from roughly **1,000 players** to **at least 10,000**. The update is set to add two dungeons and **Rako**, a Dark Matter Zee that players can earn for free through the story. Each player receives one, and anyone who wants to evolve it will need to trade with other players. That gives the story reward a purpose beyond the quest itself.

FOREST's **Stack** ran into its first major problem as a skill-based competition with prizes. The team suspects cheating affected much of the top 100 and is investigating. The first SDK is live. The partner's next game is expected to emphasize strategy and resource management, providing a way to test more complete player balance and settlement features. [April 23 Stack campaign announcement](https://forest.inc/news/stack-10-000-play-campaign-by-acid-gamez) [April 30 Forest SDK announcement](https://forest.inc/news/forest-sdk-infra)

[14:40](#t=880)

Jackal said the team planned to submit the build that week and aimed to release it the following week. Euler's estimate at the time was **Monday, May 11**. The first quest was called **Back in One Piece** on the call. During development, someone used *Back in Two Piece* as a placeholder for the follow-up quest, a pun Jackal liked. **Post-call verification.** The official quest name in version 1.11.0, released May 11, was **A Longer Ask**. The announcement also confirmed Rako and the new dungeon bosses. Version 1.11.1 on May 13 began addressing issues with the main quest and The Well. [May 11 version 1.11.0 release notes](https://zeeverse.com/news/1-11-0-release-notes) [May 13 version 1.11.1 release notes](https://zeeverse.com/news/1-11-1-release-notes)

- **The Mountainside** is the first dungeon, previously known among players as the Grass Dungeon.
- **The Overgrowth** and **The Well** are the next two dungeons. Small challenges near the entrances give new Shamans a chance to practice. Jackal said this part adds three NPCs who act as checkpoints and guides. An in-development view briefly showed four characters.
- The team showed the stage selection and wave-based combat setting for **The Well**. The visuals were lighting tests designed for default or low mobile graphics settings. Euler said the team used the opportunity to revisit combat environments and animations, some of which dated back to earlier versions. ## 2. Each player can claim one Rako, and evolution requires three of the same Zee

[15:36](#t=936)

Jackal said the main quest had previously offered nine starter Zee options for free. Many other Zees came from collaborations or special releases. The raccoon-like **Rako** is what he called the tenth Zee obtainable through regular play. It is a **Dark Matter** type. The active ability of its first form blinds an opponent. The speakers compared it with an ability from the Zoomba line and specified that Rako's version only blinds. Its evolved form gains stealth, which they compared with Chomp's evolved line. The call gave no ability names, hit rates, or other exact stats. [Zeedex for existing Zee names and evolutions](https://whitepaper.zee-verse.com/gameplay/zee/zeedex) [June 11 version 1.13.0 release notes, which later confirmed the spelling of Rako Egg](https://zeeverse.com/news/1-13-0-release-notes)

The plan was to release Rako's first form through the main quest and follow with the evolution about a week later. Staggering the releases would give players time to train their newly acquired Rako. Euler emphasized that each player gains one from the quest and that evolution takes **three Rakos in total**, including the one being evolved. Players need to find others willing to part with two more and train the Zees involved to the required level. Asked about a second evolution, Jackal pointed to the game's **level 99 cap** at the time and said players would only need to consider one evolution in the near term. The general game documentation explains that once a Zee reaches level 50, evolution uses two Zees of the same type as materials. The level cap and Rako's acquisition rules above reflect what was said on the call. [Zee evolution documentation](https://whitepaper.zee-verse.com/gameplay/zee)

The hosts discussed releasing Rako's evolution later. Euler then explained why training the one awarded by the quest would only get a player partway there.

> **Euler** · [23:45](#t=1425)
>
> “In order to evolve Raccoon, you need three freaking Raccoons. Do you understand that? And the beauty of this is that, uh, if I'm not mistaken, uh, there you can only get one raccoon by completing the quest. \[…\] So this kind of brings in this interesting dilemma where you will have to find a bunch of other players who have gotten them.”

**Post-call verification.** The version 1.12.0 announcement on the official website on May 22 listed the Rako evolution and the off-chain Marketplace, where players can browse, buy, and list items and Zees. The App Store dates this version to May 19. [May 22 version 1.12.0 release notes](https://zeeverse.com/news/1-12-0-release-notes) [App Store version history](https://apps.apple.com/lv/app/zeeverse/id6755680403?platform=mac)

## 3. The second recruitment round targets at least 10,000 players, and Daily Rewards will stay

[28:17](#t=1697)

A player asked whether user acquisition had resumed. Euler said the team had spent most of the prior two weeks finishing the second and third quests and had yet to bring in another cohort. The previous batch brought in around **1,000 new players**. Those who got into the game often completed the available campaign in **one to two hours**. They ran out of content so early that the team struggled to tell what they might do the following day. Once all three quests are ready, the team plans to test with at least **10,000 players** and watch how quickly they finish and what they do beyond the quests. The team had fixed a number of smaller issues from the first test and shared no new next-day retention figures on the call. [April 20 version 1.10.0 release notes](https://zeeverse.com/news/1-10-0-release-notes)

A player asked for new onboarding data. Euler explained why the team wanted all three quests ready before expanding acquisition.

> **Euler** · [28:46](#t=1726)
>
> “Engaged users actually went through the whole, uh, campaign part one too quickly for us to be even able to judge what happens after one day, because most of them just played through the whole thing in in like one or two hours, and and they have naturally question what next.”

Players asked to keep **Daily Rewards**. Euler joked about removing something everyone liked, then confirmed the current rewards would stay. The team was interested in incentives that give people a reason to return after the first week, with a monthly calendar and special rewards among the ideas still under discussion.

## 4. Heist needs UI work and bug fixes before its return, with 3v3 versus 5v5 first up for debate

[32:25](#t=1945)

Heist had been disabled before the game-wide UI redesign. Bringing it back still required UI work, several bug fixes, and **a reset of every player's Elo**. Jackal said the main quest and Forge remained development priorities. Heist was most likely to return after the main quest update, with **1.12** mentioned as a possibility. The team intended to use its limited development time to get the mode running first, then revise the rules with player feedback. The later 1.12.0 notes confirmed Rako's evolution and the off-chain Marketplace. A return for Heist still awaited a separate announcement. [October 22, 2025 version 1.7.3 notes on disabling Heist](https://zeeverse.com/news/1-7-3-release-notes) [March 9 version 1.8.0 UI redesign announcement](https://zeeverse.com/news/1-8-0-release-notes) [May 22 version 1.12.0 release notes](https://zeeverse.com/news/1-12-0-release-notes)

Jackal intended **3v3 versus 5v5** to be the first topic. Further proposals would also need support from other players, and earlier rules could be revisited and rolled back. His phrase “community takeover” referred to players having a say in the rules. The team would still be responsible for implementing changes and funding buybacks. The call gave no result for a first vote and no confirmed return date.

While discussing Heist's rules after the UI changes and Elo reset, Jackal named the first question he wanted players to debate.

> **Jackal** · [33:52](#t=2032)
>
> “Should it be 3v3 or 5v5? And you know, I will be listening to a lot of discussions, and I hope we can make a discussion, instead of, you know, a lot of people staying silent, and then, you know, not being happy.”

## 5. Trading Q&A covers Stash sales and how quickly irregular activity can spread through the economy

[25:24](#t=1524)

A player asked to sell Relics in bulk from the **Stash**, then suggested a narrower option that lets the Hoarder take items directly from the Stash when buying them. Jackal said full bulk selling from the Stash was unscheduled at the time. A proposal involving tickets for some items might already be on the backlog. The team said the current **20-slot inventory** and **roughly two-minute wait tied to loot** give it time to react to irregular transfers. Euler considered allowing only the Hoarder to access the Stash for sales a possible compromise. Jackal pointed out that picking up Relics, bringing them back, and cashing them in are part of the reward experience. No decision was made on the call. [Version 1.3.0 release notes on Relics and the Hoarder](https://zeeverse.com/news/1-3-0-release-notes)

**Where is the selling pressure on $VEE coming from?** Euler had yet to revisit the data in detail over the previous month. He suspected that vested rewards were still being released from an older staking contract. Jackal mentioned that the Ambassador Program ended on January 29, and Euler said those rewards had already been airdropped. The discussion centered on a separate Legacy staking contract. The speakers gave differing years and dates for when it stopped awarding rewards. The references to “45 days after January 19” and “March 5” remain unverified. Euler believed prior withdrawal batches had settled. He also said the team had seen players use deposits and withdrawals to move funds between **Arbitrum and Ronin**. A small withdrawal fee was under consideration, with the amount and effective date still open. [Official $VEE documentation on uses, deposits, and withdrawals](https://whitepaper.zee-verse.com/economy/utility-tokens/usdvee)

**What happens if gold farmers sell items into GEX and then dump $VEE?** Euler said the team had seen limited activity of this kind. Each item has its own liquidity pool, so heavy selling into one pool first pushes down the price of that item. The impact on the wider economy depends on pool size and trading volume. The call gave no monitoring report, transaction totals, or contract flow data. [Official GEX documentation](https://whitepaper.zee-verse.com/economy/game-exchange-gex)

## 6. FOREST investigates Stack's leaderboard and takes the SDK toward in-game settlement

[02:43](#t=163)

### Stack's prizes prompted a leaderboard investigation

[02:51](#t=171)

Stack is a timing game about stacking blocks. After receiving reports of a leaderboard anomaly, Euler suspected that unusual scores occupied almost the entire top 100. The partner was investigating and planned to remove scores found to be invalid and explain where the prize money would go. The investigation had yet to yield a published final list, standards for what counted as a valid score, or payout results. The partner had also started preparing another game with more emphasis on **strategy, planning, and allocating resources**. Jackal brought up abuse seen in the early Harvester game and an internal tournament test. Reviving it was still an idea. [Stack campaign announcement, with the first season ending May 7](https://forest.inc/news/stack-10-000-play-campaign-by-acid-gamez)

After anomalies appeared on the partner game's Stack leaderboard, Euler explained the team's suspicion and the state of the investigation.

> **Euler** · [03:11](#t=191)
>
> “If it's a skill game, most likely, people will be able to find a way to cheat it, and I mean this probably this case was no different. So, uh, we are pretty much like investigating all that.”

### The first SDK is live, and the Agent Skill and balance operations are in development

[04:40](#t=280)

Euler said the first **Forest SDK** had come out the previous week. HTML5 developers can read a connected wallet and token balances and start swaps from the game interface. The next step is to let apps use players' in-game balances to handle spending and settle rewards. Euler gave the example of a farming game where players spend tokens to build a farm, then use the tokens it produces for upgrades or swap them. The team planned to package the integration into an **Agent Skill** so creators using agents such as ChatGPT and Claude can connect wallets, tokens, and settlement. The partner's new game might be among the first to use these features. [April 30 Forest SDK announcement](https://forest.inc/news/forest-sdk-infra)

After discussing the first SDK, Euler described how creators would integrate features such as user balances in the next phase.

> **Euler** · [11:22](#t=682)
>
> “Anyone who have access to ChatGPT or Claude will be able to just enable the skill, and your agent will exactly know how to build an app you describe, and seamlessly integrate, uh, the support for m-, manipulating with user balances, initiating swaps, reading the wallets, and and so on.”

**Post-call verification.** The May 23 **Forest Protocol Playkit** announcement described game balances, reward vaults, settlement protections, and the Agent Skill. This was the public date for those additional features. [May 23 Playkit announcement](https://forest.inc/news/forest-protocol-the-settlemeny-layer-for-on-chain-games)

## 7. Other Q&A, a community shoot, and the closing raffle

[44:36](#t=2676)

- **Will the Amberwing quest return?** Jackal said he had written a version that brought back Amberwing and connected the story to Forge. The team later chose to link the Great Tree to Forge first, leaving the Amberwing quest on hold. Ideally, he would like to make it a fuller quest. A World Boss version had been considered, with the final format and timing still open.
- **Drop rates and uses for high-tier Umbra materials.** Jackal said drop rates would stay as planned at the time. **Mithril, Adamantite, and Oceanite** will be used to make higher-tier tools. The plan was to make the tools craftable first, then work on mining on player-owned Land. The tools were expected to unlock through Crafting recipes. The team gave no Blueprint requirement on the call. [Version 1.7.1 release notes on changing Umbra drops to ores](https://zeeverse.com/news/1-7-1-release-notes)
- **When will Land become a development priority?** The team planned to revisit Land after the new-player experience stabilized. Euler felt it would make sense to test how to open it once player numbers kept growing and players started looking for smaller communities. The call gave no public date or production rules.
- **Are there new plans for Zeeverse Mini?** Euler liked the project and felt a FOREST integration was worth exploring. It is also a skill-based mini-game, so automated cheating attracted by a prize pool could hurt regular players. There was no confirmed relaunch plan.
- **The next livestream will capture advertising footage.** Jackal invited players to act out dungeon runs, fights over mining spots, runs through frequently visited areas, and a charge toward the Veil of Souls. He would organize the shots. Participants could enter a raffle with several Relics and possibly a Genesis Mask mentioned as prizes. Euler estimated the ads could generate millions of impressions once they ran. They had yet to launch.
- **Closing.** The team raffled off three **one-month Premium Passes**, with winners to claim them through support tickets. Jackal said players with Genesis eligibility could give the code to someone they wanted to bring into the game. He asked players to join the shoot the following Tuesday. Euler suggested skipping a week might give them more to talk about, and the call ended without a decision to move permanently to an every-other-week schedule. ## 9. My overall assessment

The two most important threads from this call concern **using rewards to encourage the kind of play the team wants to see**. For Zeeverse, three main quests give new players more to do, while raising Rako encourages players who want its evolved form to start trading. If trading proves too hard for new players, the one-per-player distribution could leave many with only the first form. For FOREST, Stack showed that people are willing to compete for prizes and revealed the cost of verifying a skill-based leaderboard. Whether the next strategy game can settle rewards fairly depends on how placements, payouts, and duplicate accounts are handled.

The next things to watch are **completion and next-day return rates for each of the three quests, how readily players can find the materials to evolve Rako, the result of Heist's first rules vote, and what Stack actually pays out after investigating the leaderboard**.
